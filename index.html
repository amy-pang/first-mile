          Before you drive

          Massachusetts rules and signs, explained

          Everything the simulation checks, in plain language. Read it once, then drive the run and watch for each sign.

          Driving a rotary, step by step

          1. Merge out of the ending lane. Signal left, check your left mirror, then look over your shoulder for a car in your blind spot. Move over only into a safe gap.
          2. Slow down early and count your exit. The green diagram sign shows every exit with its route shield. Know whether yours is the 1st, 2nd or 3rd before you arrive.
          3. Watch the crosswalk. If someone is crossing, stop before the white bars. People in a crosswalk have the right of way.
          4. Yield at the white triangles and look left. Cars already in the rotary have the right of way, and they come at you from the left.
          5. Turn right into a safe gap. Traffic in a rotary always travels counterclockwise. Never turn left into it.
          6. Keep moving at a low speed. Around 20 mph is plenty. Don't stop inside the rotary unless something blocks you.
          7. Signal right, then exit. Turn on your right signal after you pass the exit before yours, so drivers waiting to enter know you are leaving.

          Other Massachusetts rules newcomers miss

          • Hands-free only. Since February 2020, drivers may not hold a phone while driving. Mount it and use voice or single-tap controls.
          • Yield to emergency vehicles. When you hear a siren or see flashing lights, let the vehicle go first. In a rotary, don't enter in front of it and don't stop inside the circle: exit, then pull to the right and slow down or stop until it passes.
          • Wipers on, headlights on. Whenever your windshield wipers are running, your headlights must be on too.
          • Default speed limits. Boston's default is 25 mph unless posted. Elsewhere, thickly settled areas default to 30 mph.
          • Right on red is allowed after a full stop, unless a "No Turn on Red" sign is posted. Boston has many of them.
          • Yellow means advice, white means law. Yellow speed plaques are advisory; white speed limit signs are the legal maximum.

          How your score works

          Your score is out of 100 and comes from how you drive, not whether you reach your exit. Steady habits lose points for every second they slip, more the further off you are. One-off rules lose a set amount. You pass with 80 or more and no critical error. Running late never costs points.

            Critical errors fail the run whatever the score, as on a road test: any collision, driving through an occupied crosswalk, failing to yield entering the rotary, pulling out in front of an ambulance, or changing lanes into a car in your blind spot.
